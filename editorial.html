Editorial standards and correction process

How AirPath Lab selects topics, verifies sources and corrects errors.

Source selection

We prioritize primary sources such as the US Environmental Protection Agency, CDC/NIOSH, ENERGY STAR and national health or technical agencies for definitions, sizing examples and hazard limitations. Where a manufacturer’s device details are involved, a current manufacturer manual must govern operation and maintenance. We link supporting sources near articles rather than asking readers to trust generic statements.

What an article must explain

Each article names the specific concern, separates causes from observations, suggests low-risk steps, gives an example, and identifies when professional help is appropriate. We do not fabricate product tests, imply certifications, use fake testimonials or promise a numerical threshold that will make every room safe. Our tools show units and model assumptions.

Time-sensitive guidance

Standards Watch articles are explicitly dated and point to the current official source. They are not an automatically updated news service. Recommendations and regulatory requirements change across jurisdictions, so readers must check the live governing documents before relying on a dated summary.

Corrections

We correct factual errors, broken source links and misleading formulas when identified. Send the URL and a concise explanation to rafligranada@gmail.com. The project does not claim external professional review or independent lab validation unless such work is explicitly documented on the relevant page.

Advertising separation

Advertising may be present on eligible pages through Google AdSense. Ads are not editorial endorsements. We do not rank brands in exchange for payment. See Advertising disclosure.

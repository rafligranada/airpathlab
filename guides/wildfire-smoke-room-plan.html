Seasonal · GUIDE 12

When outdoor smoke is high: adjust the indoor air plan

A room-by-room checklist that prioritizes official alerts and well-fitted filtration.

AirPath Lab EditorialPublished and reviewed: 12 Nov 2026Reading time: 5–7 min
Safety boundary. This page explains observations and planning. It does not diagnose indoor pollution, replace building-code assessment or provide wiring, fuel-gas or mold-remediation instructions. Follow equipment manuals and seek qualified help for hazards.

Check official conditions before ventilation

Wildfire smoke conditions change quickly. Follow local air-quality advisories and emergency instructions rather than making decisions from an old blog forecast. During smoke events, bringing outdoor air inside may increase indoor particles. If you must evacuate, follow officials; an air cleaner does not make an unsafe building safe.

FIELD NOTE 01Write down the advisory source and the time you checked it. A visible haze indoors is not a reliable quantitative PM2.5 measurement.

Reduce indoor particle sources too

If outdoor air is contaminated, avoid adding avoidable indoor pollution through burning candles, smoky cooking or smoking. Manage kitchen exhaust and other appliances according to public guidance and manufacturer instructions. Avoid unsafe changes to combustion or building systems.

FIELD NOTE 02A source-control step reduces the load on any filtration device.

Select suitable particle filtration

EPA recommends high-efficiency HVAC filters compatible with the system or portable air cleaners with adequate smoke CADR. For a typical 8-foot ceiling, a portable cleaner with smoke CADR in cfm at least about two-thirds of floor area in square feet is a rough screening approach.

FIELD NOTE 03Check real room size and keep the device running at a usable speed. A purifier designed for a closet may be insufficient for an open living area.

Keep ozone concerns in mind

Avoid devices that intentionally produce ozone in occupied indoor spaces. Do not trust a seller’s unverified “smoke elimination” statement without checking independent performance information. Some ionizing devices may pose ozone concerns depending on design.

FIELD NOTE 04Follow the manual for filter replacement and safe placement. Leave pathways unobstructed.

Know the limits

Portable cleaners do not remove all pollutants and do not protect against every hazard associated with wildfire. If power is unreliable or the building cannot reasonably limit smoke entry, follow local public-health options for cleaner-air locations.

FIELD NOTE 05Revisit the plan when outdoor conditions improve; the appropriate ventilation strategy may then change.

Practical next step

Choose one low-risk observation from this guide, write down when and where you tried it, and compare with a similar situation. If you cannot separate the cause from the result, pause before buying equipment. For a larger or persistent building problem, an in-person assessment may be the most useful next step.

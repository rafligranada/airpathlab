Diagnosis · GUIDE 18

PM2.5 monitor readings: trends, placement and limitations

A particle trend can be useful without turning the device into a regulatory monitor.

AirPath Lab EditorialPublished and reviewed: 12 Nov 2026Reading time: 5–7 min
Safety boundary. This page explains observations and planning. It does not diagnose indoor pollution, replace building-code assessment or provide wiring, fuel-gas or mold-remediation instructions. Follow equipment manuals and seek qualified help for hazards.

Check what the device reports

Many affordable sensors report PM2.5 concentration estimates based on scattered light. Calibration, humidity, particle composition and placement can affect accuracy. A reported number is not automatically equivalent to a regulatory-grade measurement.

FIELD NOTE 01Read the device’s documentation and note units, averaging period and maintenance advice.

Look for trends around activities

If a sensor rises during cooking and falls after source control and suitable filtration, the timing may be informative. But a single spike can also reflect nearby movement, cleaning or sensor artifacts. Record what was happening and repeat observations under comparable conditions.

FIELD NOTE 02Do not place the sensor in the direct outlet stream of an air cleaner and assume it represents the entire room.

Relate indoor to outdoor conditions

Outdoor smoke or traffic can influence indoor readings, depending on air exchange. Compare against trusted local outdoor advisories when possible, but remember measurements may use different instruments and averaging periods.

FIELD NOTE 03Opening a window can change an indoor PM trend for better or worse.

Avoid diagnosing health from one reading

Air pollution can have health consequences, but a consumer monitor cannot diagnose an illness or determine a safe exposure for every person. People with concerns should follow public-health guidance and seek healthcare advice as appropriate.

FIELD NOTE 04Do not claim that a reading of zero means zero indoor contaminants.

Use the data to test a plan

Combine source notes, cleaner runtime, filter maintenance and sensor trends. Keep an honest log of what is estimated versus measured. If conditions repeatedly remain poor, consider professional evaluation and inspect possible sources.

FIELD NOTE 05The most useful result is a clearer decision, not a decorative dashboard number.

Practical next step

Choose one low-risk observation from this guide, write down when and where you tried it, and compare with a similar situation. If you cannot separate the cause from the result, pause before buying equipment. For a larger or persistent building problem, an in-person assessment may be the most useful next step.

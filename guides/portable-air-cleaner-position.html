Filtration · GUIDE 07

Place a portable air cleaner so its rated airflow can work

Avoid common placement mistakes without blocking exits or creating uncomfortable drafts.

AirPath Lab EditorialPublished and reviewed: 12 Nov 2026Reading time: 5–7 min
Safety boundary. This page explains observations and planning. It does not diagnose indoor pollution, replace building-code assessment or provide wiring, fuel-gas or mold-remediation instructions. Follow equipment manuals and seek qualified help for hazards.

Start with occupied time

A bedroom or living area where people spend many hours may make a better first location than a hallway. Air cleaners are designed and rated with particular room dimensions in mind. If you move one unit between rooms, each room has a different volume and potentially different pollution sources.

FIELD NOTE 01Use the product’s manual for minimum wall clearance and the preferred position; not every model draws or discharges air in the same direction.

Give the intake and outlet space

Curtains, cabinets and stacks of boxes near an intake can restrict useful flow. A cleaner squeezed beneath a desk may also increase noise and lead you to use a lower speed. Follow manufacturer clearance guidance and keep cords away from walkways.

FIELD NOTE 02Do not put any electrical appliance in a wet or unsafe location, near an open flame or where it obstructs a doorway.

Find a tolerable operating mode

Rated CADR is often determined at maximum speed. A quieter night setting may deliver less cleaned air, which is a legitimate comfort tradeoff but should be accounted for when planning. If a product publishes speed-specific performance, use the setting you actually expect to run.

FIELD NOTE 03An occasionally used high-capacity unit may not offer the same exposure reduction as a suitably operated cleaner used for longer.

Interpret odor complaints correctly

Most particle filters are not designed to remove gases. A charcoal layer may address some odors under some conditions, but it can saturate, and there is no widely used comparable gas-CADR rating. Mustiness from moisture and fumes from a source require source management.

FIELD NOTE 04If you smell gas or suspect carbon monoxide, prioritize safety procedures and professional assistance rather than repositioning an air purifier.

Recheck after a week

Note times of operation, fan speed, major indoor activities, noise tolerance and whether the unit remains unobstructed. A simple weekly review reveals whether the plan was practical. If family members repeatedly unplug the unit because it is too loud, revise the plan.

FIELD NOTE 05Avoid treating one sensor reading or scent change as a proof of medical benefit.

Practical next step

Choose one low-risk observation from this guide, write down when and where you tried it, and compare with a similar situation. If you cannot separate the cause from the result, pause before buying equipment. For a larger or persistent building problem, an in-person assessment may be the most useful next step.
